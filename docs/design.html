Snack Counter Automation: Design

Framsikt Product Engineer take-home · Arijit Dutta · 15 November 2026
Idea in one line: automate the coordination, not the shopping. Every empty box gets exactly one named owner at any moment, with a deadline. If that person doesn't act, the system moves on automatically. Budget, HR claim and report all hang off that one refill record. People never leave the chat tool they already use.

1. How a refill moves from "empty" to "reimbursed"

  1. Report. Anyone types /snack empty (or scans a QR code on the box). If a refill is already open, they are told who is on it and nothing new starts.
  2. Offer. If budget is left, the bot DMs one volunteer chosen by the rules below: accept or decline within 3 h. No answer or a decline means the next person. After 3 misses, it is posted to the office channel and the coordinator is told.
  3. Commit. The first valid accept wins. The spend cap is reserved, the channel sees "Lars is refilling, don't buy", and the buyer has 24 h (reminder at 20 h). If they miss it, the reservation is released and the refill is re-offered.
  4. Receipt. Photo + amount in chat, and the box is full again. At or under the cap, the claim goes to HR immediately. Above the cap, the coordinator approves first.
  5. Reimburse. The bot polls HR, tells the volunteer at each step, and nudges the coordinator if a claim is pending for 5 days.

Nobody knows spend or what gets eatenEvery purchase lands in the ledger. The monthly report shows spend vs budget per office in its own currency, one NOK total, refills per person, most-bought items, hours a box sat empty, and days a full box lasts.

Budget check. remaining = budget − spent − reserved, per office, in its own currency, per month in the office's local time zone. On accept, cap = min(typical max, remaining) is reserved. A receipt at or under the cap is spent and claimed automatically. Above the cap, the coordinator reviews. If less than the typical minimum is left when the box is reported, the refill is blocked until the coordinator approves; an approved overspend shows as a negative remainder. Money is stored as integer øre/paise; mixing NOK and INR raises an error.

4. Assumptions (questions asked; answers not in before the deadline)

  1. Report currency is NOK, using one stored rate per month (e.g. the Norges Bank month-end rate), printed in the report so it can be reproduced.
  2. Purchases count against the office whose box was refilled, in that office's currency. A visitor's claim goes to HR in the receipt currency, and HR pays them in their payroll currency.
  3. Budget used up: refills pause. The office coordinator can approve one more.
  1. HR offers a status lookup and accepts an idempotency key. If not, the volunteer marks "paid" in chat; nothing else changes.
  2. Chat status shows office / remote / leave; visitors set /snack here oslo.
  3. Each office has one coordinator (office manager) for escalations and approvals.

5. Trade-offs

  • Asking one person at a time is slower than broadcasting (worst case about 9 h before it opens to everyone), but it removes the bystander effect that causes the problem. Broadcast stays as the fallback.
  • Reserving the full cap can block a refill near month end that would have fit. It is simple and never overspends silently, and the coordinator can override.
  • One FX rate per month makes the NOK total approximate compared with what payroll actually paid. It is fine for a management overview and reproducible. Budgets themselves never need conversion.
  • Timers run on wall-clock time, so an evening report waits for the morning. Pausing timers outside office hours is the first thing I'd add.
  • We can't make HR faster. We remove our own delay (instant submission) and make HR's delay visible.
  • A late buyer is refused once the refill is reassigned (it would cause a double buy). They are told to contact the coordinator, a rare and explicit manual case.
  • Presence trusts chat status. If it is wrong, the person declines or the offer times out, so it corrects itself.
  • The take-home stores data in memory behind a lock. Production would be one small service + Postgres, with the chat bot as a thin adapter that parses commands and calls these same methods.

6. What I would deliberately not build

  • Sensors / smart shelves to detect an empty box: people already notice, and one chat command costs nothing.
  • Receipt OCR: the volunteer types the amount, and HR checks the image anyway.
  • Per-item inventory or consumption tracking: "items bought" plus "how long a box lasts" gives the signal at near-zero effort. A one-tap monthly snack poll could come later.
  • A web app or dashboard, or live FX feeds: everyone lives in chat, and management asked for one monthly report.
  • Company cards or paying suppliers directly: that is a finance-policy change, not software. It is worth proposing, though, because it would remove reimbursement entirely.
